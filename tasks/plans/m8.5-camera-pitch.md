# Plan: M8.5 step 4b, camera pitch

## Context
The owner accepted step 4 on 2026-10-12. On the hills they found that the camera can't look up or down, which makes
navigating hard: you can't look at the ground at your feet, a cliff above you, or the causeway's edge. Today the
camera has yaw only (`render.rs` `follow`: `Quat::from_rotation_y(pose.yaw)`). Mouse look exists (a left click in
the view locks the pointer), but `read_input` ignores the mouse's vertical motion. This decision is presentation-ARCHITECTURE VA7 ("Yaw-only
camera ... revisited with height").

**The owner's answers (2026-10-12):**
- **Controls:** the mouse is primary; keys are nice to have.
- **Limits:** none. Full 360° by 360°.
- **On a step:** if mouse look isn't locked, the view returns to straight ahead. This is configurable, so it can be
  tried.
- **Reduced motion:** my judgement (below).
- **Over the top:** the view is a free head. W, the compass and the party's facing stay with the body.
- **Eye height and FOV:** left as they are, and reviewed after walking with pitch.

Client only. No engine, data, save or protocol change. The replays don't move. `Pose` stays `x, z, yaw`, so pitch
never reaches the binder or the simulation.

## The pitch (`crates/omnis-vector/src/look.rs`, new, pure, Bevy-free)
- **`wrap_pitch(p) -> f32`:** wraps into (−π, π]. There are no limits: past ±π/2 the view is upside down, looking
  back over the head.
- **`upside_down(p) -> bool`:** true when |p| > π/2.
- **`yaw_sign(p) -> f32`:** −1 when upside down, otherwise 1. While inverted, the horizontal mouse motion is
  reversed so the picture still turns the way the mouse moves.
- **`level(p, dt, snap) -> f32`:** eases toward 0 along the shorter way (wrapped), with a time constant `LEVEL_EASE`
  of about 0.15 s. It reaches 0 at once when `snap` is set, and it snaps the last small amount (< 0.001).
- **`LevelOnStep { Unlocked, Always, Never }`**, with `levels(policy, looking) -> bool`:
  - `Unlocked`, the default and the owner's answer: levels only when mouse look is off;
  - `Always`: levels on every step;
  - `Never`: never levels.
- **Positive pitch looks up.** The camera's rotation is `Quat::from_rotation_y(yaw) * Quat::from_rotation_x(pitch)`.
- **Unit tests:**
  - wrapping, including going over the top and coming back around;
  - when the view counts as upside down, and the yaw sign;
  - the level ease takes the shorter way across ±π and settles to 0;
  - each policy, with mouse look on and off.

## The shell
**`shell/movement.rs`:**
- **State:** a resource `View { pitch: f32 }`, kept outside `Intent` and `Pose`. It starts at 0 and returns to 0 on a
  new game, a load, and a map change.
- **The mouse:** in `read_input`, while looking, `delta.y` adds `-delta.y * MOUSE_SENSITIVITY` to the pitch. Each
  frame's change is clamped to `MAX_MOUSE_YAW`, as the yaw is. Then it is wrapped. The existing `mouse_yaw` is
  multiplied by `yaw_sign(pitch)`.
- **The keys:** PageUp and PageDown, held, pitch at `TURN_SPEED`. Home levels the view at once. None of the three is
  bound today (checked again against the screens' hotkeys when built).
- **On a step:** in `advance`, when `out.events` holds an `Event::Moved` (a step, a portal or a teleport) and
  `levels(prefs.level_on_step, intent.looking)` is true, a `levelling` flag is set. While it is set, each frame applies
  `level(pitch, dt, reduced_motion)` until the pitch reaches 0. A new pitch input clears it, so a mouse move during
  the ease wins.

**`shell/controls.rs`:** a **Level** button ("Level  Home") in the side row, before Camp. It is the mouse entry point
for levelling the view (owner rule: every action has a button), and it matters most under `Never`.

**`shell/render.rs` `follow`:** reads `View` and sets the rotation to yaw then pitch. Nothing else changes:
- the fog is by ground distance;
- there is no frustum culling of our own;
- the minimap, the binder and the compass read the yaw only.

## Reduced motion (my call, to state in the docs)
- Pitch from the mouse is direct, as mouse yaw already is: the player drives it, so nothing moves on its own.
- **The automatic return to level is the only motion the game makes on its own here.** Under reduced motion it is
  instant, as the 90° turns and the settle already are (presentation-ARCHITECTURE §7.5).
- The held keys pitch at a constant rate, as the held arrow turns do.

## Settings
"Level view on step", with three choices:
- "When not mouse looking", the default;
- "Always";
- "Never".

It follows the existing six-place pattern:
- the `Prefs` field and its default;
- the `File` field, with `#[serde(default)]` so older settings files still load;
- both `From` conversions;
- a `Field` variant;
- a `choice` row;
- an `apply` arm.

The file key is `level_on_step`.

## Tests (real app, no mocks)
- **The `look.rs` unit tests** above.
- **`tests/look.rs`** (new, using `common::app`). Each test sets `View` or drives `Intent` through the real systems:
  - The camera's `Transform` looks up after a positive pitch: the forward vector has y > 0.
  - Upside down, the yaw sign is reversed.
  - With mouse look off, a step levels the view (the pitch is 0 once the ease ends; at once under reduced motion).
  - With mouse look on, a step keeps the pitch.
  - `Always` levels even while looking. `Never` keeps the pitch with mouse look off.
  - A turn with no step doesn't level.
  - The Level button and Home level the view.
  - Pitch never changes the party's facing, the yaw or the binder's commands: a full loop over the top, then a step,
    moves the party the same way as with no pitch.
- **`tests/settings.rs`:**
