# Plan: M8.5 step 1c, a stair's direction (a list of stairs, taken from the foot)

## Context
A stair has no direction. Every flight is drawn climbing north to south, and stepping onto a stair from any side
takes it. At step 3 the solid slab under a stair hid the view of anyone coming from the wrong side, so stairs stayed
lines. The owner asked for the direction now, before step 2 (2026-10-12).

**Owner's answers (2026-10-12):**
- **On the tile, in a list.** The map gains `stairs: [(level, x, y, rise)]`. One stair glyph serves every direction.
- **It changes the rules.**
  - A stair up is taken only by stepping onto it from its foot, moving the way it rises.
  - A stair down is taken only from its head, moving against the rise.
  - From any other side it is an ordinary step onto the tile.
- **Required.** Every Up or Down tile must be listed; a missing or stray entry is refused at load.

## Data (`omnis-data`)
- **The format.**
  - `MapDef.stairs: Vec<StairDef>` (`#[serde(default)]`).
  - `StairDef { level: i8 (#[serde(default)], 0 is the map's own), x: u16, y: u16, rise: Facing }`.
  - `rise` is the way the flight climbs: its foot is on the opposite side, its head on that side.
- **Resolved.** `MapData::rise(level, x, y) -> Option<Facing>`, held per level beside the cells (`LevelData`), so a
  lookup is one index.
- **Checks in `levels.rs`**, each with its message and a bad-pack corpus entry:
  - an Up or Down tile with no entry;
  - an entry on a tile that is not a stair, or outside the map, or on a level that doesn't exist;
  - two entries for one tile;
  - **an Up whose foot can't be stepped from:** the tile at `(x, y) − rise` on its level has no ground, or is
    impassable, or a wall or door stands on the edge between;
  - **a Down whose head can't be stepped from:** the same check at `(x, y) + rise`;
  - **a pair that disagrees:** an Up that lands on a Down, or a Down that lands on an Up, with a different `rise`.
    They are one flight.
- **Limits:** the entries are bounded by the map's tile count, since a tile has at most one.

## The engine (`omnis-sim/src/apply.rs`)
- **In `landing`**, the stair fires only on the right step.
  - The step's direction is `facing`, already computed there, so strafing and backing count by the way they move.
  - `joined(map, at, join, facing)`:
    - Up fires when `facing == rise`;
    - Down fires when `facing == rise.opposite()`;
    - Hole is unchanged;
    - Ladder still never fires.
  - Otherwise the step lands on the stair tile as on any floor (one `Moved`).
- **Protocol 2, unchanged in shape.** No new event, rejection or view. `docs/api.md` describes the rule under
  `Step`, the stair in the map format, and gets a changelog entry.
- **Unchanged:** saves (`SAVE_SCHEMA` 7) and the replays. The shipped maps have no stairs.

## The fixture (`crates/omnis-sim/tests/packs/levels`)
The tower's stair is moved, because a one-tile flight needs ground beyond its head on the floor above:
- **Ground (0):**
  - the stair up moves from (1, 0) to (2, 0), rising West;
  - its foot is the courtyard at (3, 0), through a new opening in the tower's east wall at row 0;
  - (1, 0) becomes stone floor.
- **Upper floor (1):** the stair down moves to (2, 0), rising West. Its head is the tower floor at (1, 0), so you go
  down by stepping East onto it from (1, 0).
- **The courtyard stair (4, 3) and the bridge's stair down rise North.** The foot is (4, 4) and the head the bridge
  at (4, 2). This already fits.
- `stairs:` lists all four tiles. The header comment says where each foot and head is.
- The engine's `tests/levels.rs` and the client's `tests/levels.rs` positions move with it.

## The client (`omnis-vector`)
- **Drawing (`geometry::joins`):** the treads cross the tile at right angles to `rise` and climb toward it, from the
  lower floor at the foot to the higher at the head. A stair pair still draws one flight (`flight`).
- **The fill comes back (`fill.rs`):** a slab under the treads, foot to head, for Up and Down. The Down tile still has
  no floor fill, being the stairwell.
- **The minimap:** a stair's mark is a chevron pointing the way it rises, for Up and Down alike, with Down keeping
  its own mark (an arrowhead toward the head with a bar at the foot, or similar; settled on the captures).
- **The mirror:** `collide.rs` does not predict joins (the binder snaps on the second `Moved`), so nothing changes.
  The agreement test still runs on every level.
