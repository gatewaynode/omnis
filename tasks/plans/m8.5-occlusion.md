# Plan: M8.5 step 3, solid occlusion (fills under the lines, then measure)

## Context
The 3D view is see-through wireframe. Every segment is drawn with immediate-mode gizmos (`shell/render.rs`), so a hill,
a wall or a floor hides nothing. 1b made this worse: the levels above and below are drawn dimmed through every floor
and ceiling. The owner could not test the stairs and ladders ("We are going to have to finish the occluding terrain
before I can really test the ladders and stairs"). presentation-PRD X13 and §5.1 ask for dark solid surfaces that hide
the lines behind them. presentation-ARCHITECTURE §8.2 plans fills beside the segments and lines as meshes, with the
line technique decided by measuring (§15.3), and the frame times recorded (§8.6, X20).

**Owner's answers (2026-10-12):**
- **Fills first, then measure.** Solid fills go under today's gizmo lines, which Bevy already depth-tests against
  meshes. Both renderers are timed. Line meshes, with a width shader, are built in this step only if the measurement
  says the gizmos are too slow; otherwise the move is deferred and the docs say so.
- **Neighbours solid.** The levels above and below get their own fills and are hidden wherever the party's level
  covers them. They show only through open air, holes, stairwells and shafts.
- **The automap stays on the cone.** presentation-PRD §12.5 stays open. Step 3 is client only: no engine, data,
  save or protocol change, and the replays don't move.

## The fills (`src/fill.rs`, new, Bevy-free)
- **The type.** `pub type Tri = [[f32; 3]; 3]`. `pub fn fills(map, map_id, level, world, ground) -> Vec<Tri>` runs
  beside `geometry::extract`, in the same world space. Fills carry no colour (§8.2).
- **Where it goes.** `geometry.rs` is 559 lines, so the fills get their own module. It reuses the geometry helpers,
  made `pub(crate)`: `span`, `sides`, `ends`, `key`, `cells_high`, `JOIN_INSET`. The edge walk (walls and doors, a
  door winning over a wall) moves into a shared `pub(crate) fn edges(...)` that both modules call, so they can't
  disagree.
- **What gets a fill:**
  - **Ground:** the four triangles of each cell's `Patch`, centre to each side's corners, exactly as
    `Ground::height` samples it. A cell with no ground on the level has none.
  - **Walls:** each wall edge's quad, from `span`'s feet to its top.
  - **Doors:**
    - a closed door is the full quad;
    - an open door is the quad minus its opening (0.2 to 0.8 across, up to the head at 85%): the lintel above it
      and the two posts' strips beside it.
  - **Blocks:** the inset box's four sides and its top, matching `boxed`.
  - **Ceilings:** a quad at the roof where the cell has a ceiling and no block, the same gate the lines use.
  - **Cliffs:** the face between the near and far rims from `sides()`, as two triangles per quarter-cell span, so
    the face follows the rims.
  - **Stairs:** the slab under the treads, `[l, floor, z0]` to `[r, to, z1]`, matching the stringers.
  - **Ladders:** none (the lines are thin).
  - **Holes:** the ring between the cell's sides and the opening inset by `JOIN_INSET`, so the inner square is open.
  - **Ramps and barriers:** no fill of their own; the ground already covers them.
- **Openings between levels**, so the stairs and ladders can be seen and used:
  - A join that leads up has **no ceiling fill**: an Up stair, or a Ladder with a level above.
  - A join that leads down has **no ground fill**: a Down stair (its slab replaces the floor), or a Ladder with a
    level below.
  - A hole keeps its ring.
  - So a stairwell or shaft is open through every level it joins, and the cellar ladder's foot keeps its floor.
  - The matching ceiling *lines* stay as they are; only the fills open.
- **Both sides draw.** The fills are drawn with culling off, so a wall hides from either side, and a ceiling hides
  from below and above.

## The scene (`src/scene.rs`)
- `Scene` gains `fills: Vec<Tri>`: the party's level and its neighbours together, since every fill looks the same.
  The neighbours' fills are built on their own `Ground`s, which are already made in `build`.
- `Scene::build` stays the only entry point, so fills are cached and rebuilt with the lines: on a change of map or
  level, and on `session.reshape` (doors).

## The renderer (`src/shell/render.rs`)
- **The mesh.** One `Mesh3d` entity holds the scene's fills, built by `fill::mesh(&[Tri]) -> Mesh`: a TriangleList
  with positions and normals. It is a pure function, so it can be tested. The mesh asset is swapped in `rebuild`.
- **The material.** An unlit `StandardMaterial`:
  - the clear colour, black, so a fill reads as the dark ground (§8.2);
  - `cull_mode: None`;
  - a `depth_bias` pushing the fills back slightly, so a wall's own edges and the floor grid are not hidden by
    their face.
  - Alternatively the bias goes on the gizmo lines (`GizmoLineConfig::depth_bias`), whichever the captures show
    without z-fighting. It is a named constant.
- **Unchanged:** the lines' gizmo drawing, the CPU fog, `NEIGHBOUR_DIM`, the quality widths and bloom. The fight's
  camera is on render layer 1, so it never sees the fills.
- **The camera.** It is unchanged, but the near plane (0.02) is checked against the depth bias in the captures.

## Measuring (§8.6)
- **The capture gains a timing mode.** `--timing` with `--screenshot` and `--walk` reports the mean and worst frame
  time over the walk to stdout, as JSON and as a line.
- **Runs.** Release build, at 1920×1080 and 5120×1440, on the hills fixture (the largest test map with relief) and
  on the town:
  - before, at HEAD (gizmos only);
  - after, with fills under the gizmos.
- **The rule for line meshes.** On this machine (the owner's Mac, offscreen, release, 5120×1440):
  - if the fills plus gizmos stay under a 16.7 ms mean and a 33 ms worst frame, line meshes are deferred: §15.3 stays
    open, with the numbers;
  - otherwise they are built in this step, as a second stage (§8.2: line meshes with a width shader, gizmos kept
    until the captures match), and I come back to you before building them.
- **Recorded, not set.** The numbers go in the review. The floor stays unset, since X20 also waits on relief and
  detail (step 5). The window's fps is read by the owner in acceptance.

## Tests (real data, no mocks)
- **`tests/fill.rs` (new):**
  - every cell with ground on every shipped map has its four ground triangles at the patch's heights; cells with no
    ground have none;
  - a wall's quad spans its feet to its top;
  - a closed door is covered at its middle, and an open door is not, but its lintel is;
  - a block's box;
  - a cliff face lies between its rims (the hills fixture);
  - a hole's centre is open and its ring is covered (the tower);
  - an Up stair cell has no ceiling fill, the matching Down stair on the level above has no ground, and a ladder
    shaft is open through every level but the bottom.
- **Occlusion along a ray (`tests/fill.rs`, or `tests/scene.rs` with a test helper):** a segment-to-triangle test
  (Möller–Trumbore) in `tests/common`:
  - from the road under the bridge, the lines of the bridge's underside are seen, and what is above the bridge is
    hidden;
  - inside the tower on the ground floor, the roof's lines are hidden, except up the ladder's shaft and the stairwell;
  - from the ground, the cellar's lines are hidden, except down the shaft;
  - on the hills, a point behind a hill is hidden from the valley;
  - on a flat shipped map, no floor-grid line is hidden from an eye standing on it, so the grid is not buried.
- **`fill::mesh`:** three vertices per triangle, the positions as given.
- **Scene:** fills come from the party's level and both neighbours; a map without levels has only its own.
- **Unchanged:** the flat-equality test of the lines still passes, since the fills are a separate output.
- **Mutations, each to be caught:**
  1. wall fills dropped;
  2. an open door filled;
  3. a stairwell's ceiling left closed;
  4. a hole's opening filled;
  5. neighbour fills dropped;
  6. ground fill on no-ground cells;
  7. the mesh drops triangles.

## Departures (as built, 2026-10-12)
- **Stairs stay lines.** The plan gave a stair a slab under its treads. In the captures, the courtyard stair's slab
  faced its walkers (who come from the south) with its high end and hid the view, because a flight always climbs north
  to south (the map has no direction for it). Stairs are lines like ladders; a stair's direction is a question for the
  owner.
- **Two 1b bugs, found by the fills, fixed red first:**
  - `Ground` gave a tile with no ground a patch at 0, so a wall beside one reached down to 0 (the bridge's railings
    stood on the road, and blocked the view from it). `Ground::patch` is now `None` there.
  - A stair down was drawn falling north to south, crossing its stair up. `geometry::flight` draws one flight from
    either end.
- **The bias went on the gizmos** (`GizmoConfig::depth_bias`, −0.002), not the fill material: it is a power of the
  depth, so it holds at grazing angles, where a constant bias does not.
- **The mesh function lives in `shell/render.rs`** (`render::mesh`), since `fill.rs` stays Bevy-free.
- **Timing:** the offscreen numbers barely move with resolution (8192×8192 is about 1.4 ms over 320×200), so they may
  understate the GPU's share; recorded with that note, and the window's reading is in acceptance.
