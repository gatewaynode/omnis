# Acceptance: M8 (subjective time and the engine's API) — closes M8

The script for your acceptance of M8 (`tasks/plans/m8-time.md`, `tasks/plans/m8-bus-crate.md`,
`tasks/plans/m8-api.md`). It covers:
- subjective time: the party's age, its shared time, and the town's date snapping on entry;
- rumors that report their age;
- the signal bus carrying reconciliation and the fight's reactions;
- the engine's API: a protocol version, tagged replies, the new views and ops, and the app reading through them.

Each step says what you will see and names the automated test that already covers it, so a failure can be
told apart from a gap. Play it in the window with a dev build (`cargo run -p omnis-app`). Parts 2 and 5 use the
MCP bridge (`.mcp.json`, restart the bridge after building): ask me to run those steps, or call the tools
yourself.

**What changed.**
- Time is the party's own. Its **age** (minutes lived) never reverses. Its **date** is what it believes the
  calendar says: in the wilds it runs on with the party, and entering a settlement sets it to the settlement's.
- Each region has a **company** (how many people gather there): the test town 900, the crossroads 700, the
  meadow 100, the dungeon 50. A minute lived counts toward the party's **shared time** at the company of where
  it was lived, and a settlement catches up by that shared time: years in the wilds are months in a town.
- The tavern's rumors happened at a minute of the town's own clock, and say how long ago.
- The app reads the game only through the engine's views (ARCHITECTURE §4.9). Nothing in play should look or
  behave differently because of that; any difference is a bug.

**What does not work yet.**
- Regions do not change while the party is away apart from their clocks: the ecosystem's catch-up is M10.
- No pack has an era other than the present.
- There is no in-game way to pass a long time except resting; part 2 passes it over MCP.

## Setup
**New game**, any party of at least two (a cleric among them for part 3), any save rule. You start in the test
town.

## Part 1: the clock line
1. Look at the HUD clock line.
   *You will see* the date, then the party's age, in the form `Year 1 day 40 14:20 night  age 2y 40d` (`night`
   only at night). Narrow the window and the age drops first, then the date shortens to `Y1 D40 14:20`.
   Covered by `omnis-app/src/panels.rs` (the clock text tests) and `ui.rs::the_clock_line_shows_the_date_and_snaps_to_the_town`.
2. Walk a few steps and open a door.
   *You will see* the minutes advance on both the date and the age, together, while in town.

## Part 2: years in the wilds, months in the town
1. Walk out through the gate (11, 2) into the meadow. Note the date and the age.
2. Ask me to pass about thirty days in the meadow (over MCP: `sim_script` with 720 short rests; the meadow's
   table never fires, so nothing interrupts it).
   *You will see* the date and the age both about thirty days on: in the wilds the party's own reckoning runs.
3. Walk back into town.
   *You will see* the date **snap back** by about twenty-seven days (the meadow's company is 100, so thirty days
   lived there are about three days shared), while the age keeps its thirty days. The age never goes back.
   Covered by `time.rs::years_in_the_wilds_are_months_in_the_town_and_the_date_snaps_back`,
   `time.rs::shared_time_is_lived_time_at_the_company_of_where_it_was_lived` and `ui.rs`'s clock test.
4. Over MCP, `time_clocks`.
   *You will see* the party's age and shared time, its date, a clock for each region met (the town, the meadow,
   and the crossroads, which the town is coupled to although it has no map), and each contact.
   Covered by `ops.rs` and `time.rs::a_second_visit_counts_only_the_time_since_the_last`.

## Part 3: rumors with an age
1. In town, go into the tavern (4, 3) and buy rumors.
   *You will see* only rumors whose time has come on the town's clock, each saying how long ago:
   "Talk from today: …", "Talk from 2 days ago: …". Right after a new game only the first can be told; after
   part 2 the town's clock has moved on and later ones appear.
   Covered by `services.rs::the_tavern_sells_food_and_tells_rumors` and `text.rs`'s `ago_text` tests.

## Part 4: a fight saved mid-way keeps its reactions
1. Declare a reaction (SHEET → TACTICS) for a member who has one, such as the wizard's Shield.
2. Start a fight (the dungeon's rats at (3, 8)), save from the pause menu during it, then load.
   *You will see* the fight go on where it was, and the declared reaction still answer an attack: the log
   names the reaction when Shield turns a hit into a miss.
   Covered by `reactions.rs::a_fight_saved_before_the_bus_keeps_its_reactions`,
   `save_and_replay.rs::a_schema_6_save_migrates` and
   `reactions.rs::reactions_answer_through_the_battle_subscription_held_for_the_fight`.

## Part 5: the engine's API
1. Over MCP, `game_status`.
   *You will see* `"reply": "status"`, `"protocol": 1`, and the new `may_save`, `seed` and `settings` beside the
   mode, the position and the date.
   Covered by `omnis-mcp/tests/replies.rs::every_reply_names_itself_and_reads_back_without_its_op`.
2. In the meadow, `rest_get` and `cast_get`.
   *You will see* each member's hit dice and whether a rest is refused, then each caster's spells castable
   outside a fight with their cost and any refusal; calling them changes nothing (`game_status`'s fingerprint
   is the same before and after).
   Covered by `api_views.rs::a_cast_row_s_refusal_is_the_command_s_and_its_effect_shows_its_time_left` and
   `api_views.rs::here_is_the_status_without_the_fingerprint_and_no_view_changes_the_world`.
3. `party_get` after a cleric casts Bless.
   *You will see* the sheet's numbers (saves, skills, proficiency, age in years) and each effect with its caster
   and minutes left.
   Covered by `api_views.rs::the_sheet_s_numbers_are_the_srd_s`.
4. Read `docs/api.md`. It is the reference for other tracks: the two tiers, every op, reply, view, command,
   event and refusal, the limits, the versions and the changelog.
   Covered by `omnis-mcp/tests/api_doc.rs` (fails when the document misses a name).

## Part 6: the app plays as before
The app now reads the game only through views. Check the places that changed most:
1. **The sheet** (SHEET): scores, saves, skills, hit dice, effects with time left, the age in years.
2. **Spells outside a fight** (SPELLS): each spell's cost and why it is grey.
3. **A fight**: a stack your member cannot reach is grey with its reason; Cunning Action offers exchange and
   hide; the bribe's price before the fight.
4. **A service**: the smith's stock and sale rows, the guild's spells.
5. **The debug panel**: flags, gold, food and the stacks.
*You will see* each exactly as before M8 step 8.
Covered by the app's tests (`omnis-app/tests/*.rs` and the in-source tests), unchanged in what they assert.
